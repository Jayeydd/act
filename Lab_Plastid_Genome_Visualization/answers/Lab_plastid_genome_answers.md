# Lab Plastid Genome Answers — Ginkgo biloba (MN443423.1)

1. **What is the scientific name of your chosen plant and its plastid genome accession number?**
   * **Scientific Name:** *Ginkgo biloba* L.
   * **Accession Number:** MN443423.1

2. **What is the total length of the plastid genome in base pairs?**
   * 156,990 base pairs (bp)

3. **Can you identify the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions on your map? Briefly describe their positions.**
   * **LSC (Large Single-Copy):** The large outer arc comprising 88,923 bp.
   * **SSC (Small Single-Copy):** The smaller single-copy arc comprising 18,261 bp.
   * **IRa & IRb (Inverted Repeats):** The two identical segments separating LSC and SSC, each measuring 24,903 bp.

4. **Give three examples of genes located in the LSC region.**
   * *psbA*, *rbcL*, and *atpB*

5. **Give at least one example of a gene located in the SSC region.**
   * *ndhF* (also *ycf1*, *rps15*)

6. **Give at least one example of a gene that occurs within an inverted repeat region. Is the gene shown more than once because of the duplicated IR regions?**
   * **Example Gene:** *rrn16* (or *rrn23*, *rps7*)
   * **Duplication:** Yes, these genes are shown twice on the circular map because they reside within the duplicated IRa and IRb segments.

7. **Choose one photosynthesis-related gene visible on your map. State its gene name and its general biological function.**
   * **Gene Name:** *psbA*
   * **Biological Function:** Encodes the D1 reaction center protein of Photosystem II, essential for light absorption and water oxidation during photosynthesis.

8. **What does the direction of the gene arrows or gene orientation tell you about transcription on the plastid genome?**
   * Arrows pointing clockwise indicate transcription on the outer strand, while counter-clockwise arrows indicate transcription on the inner strand.

9. **Look at the GC content graph. Does GC content appear uniform across the entire plastid genome, or does it vary among regions? Describe what you observe without over-interpreting it.**
   * GC content is non-uniform across the genome. It is noticeably higher in the IR regions (around 43%) compared to the LSC (~37%) and SSC (~34%) regions, primarily due to high GC density in ribosomal RNA genes (*rrn16*, *rrn23*, *rrn4.5*, *rrn5*).

10. **Why is a graphical plastid genome map useful compared with viewing only the raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations?**
    * A circular graphical map provides an intuitive visual overview of genome architecture, gene clustering, boundaries between structural regions (LSC/SSC/IR), transcription orientation, and GC variation, which are difficult to extract from raw sequence text or long tables.
